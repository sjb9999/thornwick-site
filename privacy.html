Thornwick: Garden Merge
A cosy story merge game by Littlehollow Games

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026

The short version. Thornwick has no accounts and never asks for your name, email or anything like it. Your progress stays on your own device, and its reminders are set on your phone itself. The game shows ads from Google AdMob, which collects some information about your device to show and measure those ads, and in the UK, EU and Switzerland it asks your permission first. If you buy anything in the game, Apple (on iPhone) or Google Play (on Android) handles the payment.

Who makes Thornwick

Thornwick: Garden Merge ("the game") is made by Littlehollow Games, an independent developer based in the United Kingdom, called "we" on this page. We're responsible for the game and this policy. Google is separately responsible for the information its ad service collects (see below). If you have any question about this policy or your information, email fernhollowgame@gmail.com.

Your game progress

Your board, gardens, coins, Gold Leaf, tea, decorations, Album, finds and settings are saved on your device only. They may also be included in your phone's own backups (for example iCloud or Google's Android backup), which we can't see. We have no servers that receive your progress, and deleting the app deletes it.

Reminders

If you allow it, the game sets gentle reminders on your phone (for example when your tea flask is full again, or a garden bed is ready to pick). They're scheduled by your phone itself: nothing is sent to us or anyone else. You can switch them off at any time in the game's Menu (Alerts) or in your phone's Settings.

Ads (Google AdMob)

The game is free because it shows ads: a short break ad now and then, and optional videos you can choose to watch for a reward (some tea for your flask, or to double a chest). These ads come from Google AdMob. To show them, Google may collect:

  • your device's advertising identifier: on iPhone only if you allow tracking when Apple's "Allow tracking?" message appears; on Android, unless you've deleted it in your phone's settings
  • on Android, an "app set ID", which lets Google count ads across apps from the same developer
  • your IP address, and a rough location worked out from it (country or city, never your precise location)
  • your device type, operating system and app version
  • how you interact with the game and its ads: when the game is opened, and which ads were shown, watched or tapped
  • diagnostic information, such as crash logs and how quickly ads load

Google uses this to show ads, count them, stop fraud and, only where you've given permission, choose ads that suit you. Google may process this information in the United States and other countries, using legal safeguards such as the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (and its UK Extension) and standard contractual clauses. You can read how in how Google uses information from apps that use its services and in Google's Privacy Policy.

Your choices:

  • In the UK, the EU and Switzerland, a message from Google asks for your consent before any personal data is used for ads. You can change your answer at any time in the game's Menu → Privacy → Ad privacy choices.
  • On iPhone, you can change your tracking choice at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.
  • On Android, you can delete your advertising ID at any time in your phone's Settings → Privacy → Ads (on some phones, Settings → Google → Ads).
  • If you say no, you'll still see ads, but they won't be chosen using your personal data or your past activity. Google may still use some information about your device, such as your IP address, to deliver each ad, count it and prevent fraud.

We ask Google for ads suitable for all ages only, with sensitive subjects (such as gambling and dating) blocked.

Why we're allowed to use this information

UK and EU data protection law asks us to say which legal reason applies:

  • Your consent for personalised ads, and for storing or reading information on your device for ads, where the law needs it. You can withdraw it at any time with the choices above.
  • Legitimate interests for basic ads that aren't personalised: showing them, counting them and preventing fraud keeps the game free, and it uses as little information as possible.
  • Providing what you asked for when you buy something in the game.

How long information is kept

We don't keep any of your information ourselves. Your progress stays on your device until you delete the game. Google keeps ad information only for limited periods set out in its policies (for example, it removes part of the IP address in its ad logs after about 9 months). See how Google retains data. Apple and Google keep purchase records as their own policies describe.

Purchases

The game offers optional purchases, such as Gold Leaf and Remove Ads. On iPhone, Apple handles the payment through the App Store; on Android, Google handles it through Google Play. We never see your card or payment details. The game only checks with Apple or Google what has been bought, so it can give it to you (and give Remove Ads back after "Restore purchases" on a new phone). See Apple's Privacy Policy and Google's Privacy Policy.

Children

Thornwick is made for players aged 13 and over and isn't aimed at children. We don't knowingly collect personal information from anyone, children included, and we ask Google for ads suitable for all ages, with sensitive ad subjects blocked. If you think a child under 13 is playing, an adult can choose "Do not consent" in the game's Menu → Privacy → Ad privacy choices (in the UK, EU and Switzerland), or turn off ad tracking in the phone's settings as described above. If you think a child's information has been shared with us, email us and we'll help.

Your rights

Under UK and EU data protection law you can ask to see, correct or delete personal information about you. As we don't keep any personal information ourselves, there's normally nothing for us to hand over, but we're happy to answer questions. For ad data, use the choices above or contact Google. If you're unhappy with how your information is handled, you can complain to the UK's Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk, or to the data protection authority where you live.

Changes to this policy

If the game changes in a way that affects your information (for example a new feature or ad partner), we'll update this page and the date at the top.
